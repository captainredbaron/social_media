Caption:
A recovery is not measured only by what the employee says.

It is measured by how quickly the customer feels the business has taken ownership.

Tax, Brown and Chandrashekaran showed that complaint handling is judged through fairness: outcome, process and interaction.

That sounds academic until you watch it happen in a real visit.

A guest can accept a mistake.
A guest can accept an apology.
A guest can even accept a practical limitation.

What they do not accept is uncertainty.

When the apology is fast but the action is slow, confidence keeps draining.
When the manager is polite but has no authority, the recovery feels decorative.
When the customer has to ask twice what happens next, the clock has already become part of the failure.

This is why I believe mystery shopping should measure the recovery clock, not just the recovery script.

How long does it take your operation to move from apology to ownership?

## Thursday, July 30 - Field File
Topic: Late Ownership
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-07-28/thursday-field-file-late-ownership.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the apology that arrived first, but ownership arrived late.

In one anonymized visit, the guest raised a simple service issue.

The frontline response was polite.
The apology was immediate.
The tone was professional.

But then the recovery stalled.

The employee needed approval. The manager was not nearby. The guest waited without a clear next step.

Nothing in the interaction was hostile. That is what made the finding useful.

The weak point was not attitude. It was authority.

A recovery process that depends on finding the right person is not really a process. It is a search.

In my view, the first recovery question is not, 'Did we apologize?'

It is: 'How quickly did the guest know we had taken ownership?'

Where does your recovery process lose time: decision, approval or communication?

Caption:
Most companies measure the service moment after it has already escaped.

A customer asks a question. The team gives an answer. The visit continues. By the time the report arrives, everyone debates memory, intention and context.

That is why evidence matters.

Kahneman showed that people do not remember experiences like a clean recording. They remember the emotional peak and the ending with disproportionate weight.

For CX leaders, this is uncomfortable and useful.

A technically acceptable visit can still leave a weak memory if the final moment feels indifferent.

A flawed visit can recover if the peak is handled with competence and the ending restores confidence.

This is why mystery shopping should not only ask whether the standard was followed. It should show which moments shaped the memory the customer carried away.

In my view, the best CX reports do not reduce a visit to a score. They explain what the customer is likely to remember, why it matters, and what management can change.

Where in your customer journey is the memory being won or lost?

## Thursday, September 10 - Data Drop
Topic: The Final 30 Seconds
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-09-08/thursday-data-drop-final-30-seconds.jpg

Caption:
Data Drop: the final 30 seconds carry more weight than teams think.

Across service audits, the closing moment is often treated as administrative.

The bill is settled.
The bag is handed over.
The advisor says goodbye.
The customer leaves.

But in the customer’s memory, the ending is not admin. It is the last signal of whether the brand still cares after the transaction is functionally complete.

This is why I like tracking the close separately from the rest of the visit.

Was there ownership?
Was the customer acknowledged?
Was the next step clear?
Did the employee end with confidence, not routine?

The ending does not need theatre. It needs discipline.

If the last 30 seconds feel careless, they can downgrade everything that came before.

What does your closing moment tell customers about your operating standard?

Caption:
In customer experience, consistency is usually more valuable than theatre.

One excellent visit is useful.

A repeatable standard is strategic.

Parasuraman, Zeithaml and Berry showed that reliability sits at the centre of service quality: the ability to perform the promised service dependably and accurately.

That sounds simple until you measure it across locations, shifts and employees.

A guest may forgive an ordinary moment if the brand is dependable. What they struggle to forgive is unpredictability.

One branch remembers the preference. Another asks the same question again.
One employee owns the handoff. Another lets the customer restart the story.
One shift recovers quickly. Another waits for permission.

The customer does not call this operational variance. They call it risk.

This is why I believe mystery shopping should measure repeatability, not just isolated performance. A high score in one visit means little if the standard depends on who happened to be working that day.

At GWR, we turn that pattern into evidence: people, process, timing, handoff, recovery and closure.

Because premium service is not the occasional exceptional moment. It is the confidence that the next visit will not become a gamble.

Where does your customer experience depend too much on the right employee being on duty?

## Thursday, August 27 - Field File
Topic: The Two-Branch Problem
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-08-25/thursday-field-file-two-branch-problem.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the two-branch problem.

In one anonymized visit pattern, the first location made the customer feel known.

The greeting was clear. The preference was remembered. The handoff felt controlled.

Then the same customer visited another branch of the same brand and had to start again.

Same promise. Different experience.

That is the kind of finding executives should take seriously.

The issue was not one rude employee. It was repeatability. The brand had a standard, but the customer could not rely on it travelling across the operation.

This is where mystery shopping earns its place. It does not only tell us whether a single visit was good. It shows whether the system can reproduce the standard when the manager is not watching.

A strong employee can create confidence. A weak repeat can erase it.

Where does your brand feel excellent in one location and uncertain in another?

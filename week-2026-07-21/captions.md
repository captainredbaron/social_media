Caption:
Trust does not usually collapse in one dramatic moment.

It leaks.

One repeated preference.
One promise made by one employee and lost by the next.
One apology that arrives after the guest has already adjusted their expectations.

Sirdeshmukh, Singh and Sabol showed that customer trust is built through competence, benevolence and problem-solving reliability.

In other words: the guest is constantly asking a silent question.

Can I rely on this brand to do what it said it would do?

This is why mystery shopping should not only measure whether the standard was followed.

It should also measure whether the promise survived the journey.

A greeting can pass. A waiting time can pass. A checklist can pass.

But if the guest has to repeat themselves, chase an answer, or accept a weaker version of what was promised, trust has already leaked.

In my view, the best CX reports do not just show failure. They show the exact point where confidence started to drain.

Where is trust most likely to leak in your customer journey?

## Thursday, July 23 - Field File
Topic: The Lost Preference
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-07-21/thursday-field-file-lost-preference.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the preference that disappeared.

In one anonymized visit, the guest mentioned a simple preference early in the experience.

Nothing complex. Nothing expensive. Just a small detail that should have followed them through the journey.

The first employee acknowledged it.
The second employee did not receive it.
The third employee asked the guest to repeat it.

That was the moment trust leaked.

Not because anyone was rude. Not because the team lacked effort. Because the operation had no reliable way to protect context.

This is one of the most useful things mystery shopping can reveal: where the brand promise depends on memory instead of process.

If the guest has to carry the context, the system is borrowing from their patience.

What preference, request or promise is your team most likely to lose between touchpoints?

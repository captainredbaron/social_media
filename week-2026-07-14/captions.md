Caption:
Most companies treat expectations as a fixed line.

They are not.

Berry and Parasuraman described the Zone of Tolerance as the space between what a customer considers adequate and what they actually desire.

That space is where a lot of CX management gets lazy.

If the visit lands just above adequate, the score may look acceptable. The guest may not complain. The team may assume the standard is working.

But acceptable is not the same as defensible.

In premium service, the zone narrows quickly. A short wait can be forgiven in one context and feel careless in another. A polite answer can be enough for a routine request and weak for a high-stakes problem.

This is why mystery shopping should measure more than compliance.

It should show where the experience sits: below adequate, inside tolerance, or close enough to desired that the brand can defend its promise.

In my view, the most dangerous CX result is not a failure. It is a pass that quietly trains the business to accept mediocrity.

Where is your operation confusing adequate with excellent?

## Thursday, July 16 - Data Drop
Topic: The Dangerous Pass
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-07-14/thursday-data-drop-dangerous-pass.jpg

Caption:
Data Drop: the dangerous pass.

In mystery shopping, I pay close attention to the visits that technically pass but still feel weak.

The greeting happened.
The answer was correct.
The waiting time stayed inside the standard.
The apology was delivered.

On paper, nothing collapsed.

But the guest would not describe the experience as excellent. They would describe it as acceptable.

That difference matters.

A pass tells you the minimum standard was met. It does not prove the promise was felt.

This is where the Zone of Tolerance becomes useful for operators: it separates adequate service from desired service. Both may avoid complaints. Only one builds confidence.

At GWR, I want reports to help leaders see that difference clearly. Compliance protects the floor. Evidence should also show how far the experience is from the ceiling.

What is one metric in your business that may be hiding an acceptable-but-weak experience?

Caption:
In CX, I pay close attention to the moments where the customer and the team meet.

Bitner, Booms and Tetreault studied 700 airline, hotel and restaurant incidents in 1990, and the lesson still holds: customers remember service encounters because that is where the brand becomes real.

A helpful employee.
A poor recovery.
A confusing handoff.
A small act of ownership.

These moments diagnose the system better than any slogan.

The question for me is simple: are we training teams to complete tasks, or to recognize the moments that customers will remember?

Mystery shopping is useful because it captures those encounters as evidence. Not as opinion. Not as a complaint after the fact. As the moment the brand either held together or broke in front of the customer.

What service encounter would your customers still remember a week later?

## Thursday, August 6 - Field File
Topic: The Handoff That Erased the Win
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-08-04/thursday-field-file-handoff-erased-win.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the handoff that erased the win.

In one anonymized visit, a customer asked for help with a simple issue.

The first employee handled it beautifully: calm tone, clear explanation and real ownership.

Then came the handoff.

The next touchpoint treated the customer like the conversation had never happened. Same question again. Same frustration again. The good work was erased in seconds.

This is one of the most common CX gaps I see. The problem is not always attitude. Sometimes it is continuity.

A great response is only great if the next person can carry it forward.

Where does your customer journey lose the story between one employee and the next?

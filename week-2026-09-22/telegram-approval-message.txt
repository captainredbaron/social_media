Caption:
Trust rarely disappears in one dramatic failure.

It usually leaks through small signals.

A promise that is not remembered.
A handoff that forces the customer to repeat the story.
A confident answer that later changes.
A delay with no ownership.

Each one looks minor in isolation. Together, they tell the customer something more serious: the system may not be reliable.

Mayer, Davis and Schoorman described trust as a judgement built on ability, benevolence and integrity. In service operations, those are not abstract ideas. Customers read them through evidence.

Ability: do you know what you are doing?
Benevolence: are you acting in my interest?
Integrity: do your actions match what you promised?

That is why I do not like CX reports that only say "standard met" or "standard missed." The management question is sharper: what did this moment teach the customer about whether the brand can be trusted?

At GWR, my team uses mystery shopping as an early-warning instrument. We look for the small operational signals that quietly weaken confidence before they become complaints.

Because when trust leaks, the customer may still smile, pay and leave.

They just become less certain they should come back.

Where is trust leaking quietly in your customer journey?

2. Thursday post - Field File: The customer had to repeat the story three times
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-09-22/thursday-field-file.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the customer had to repeat the story three times.

The issue was not complicated.

A customer made a request, explained the context, and was transferred to another team member.

Then the story had to be repeated.

Then repeated again.

Nobody was rude. Nobody refused to help. The final answer was acceptable.

But confidence still dropped.

Why?

Because every repeated explanation told the customer the system was not listening.

This is one of the quietest trust leaks in service operations. It rarely appears as a formal complaint, but it changes how safe the customer feels relying on the brand.

In mystery shopping, we track these handoff moments separately: was context carried forward, was ownership clear, did the next person know enough to continue without making the customer restart?

The service may finish correctly and still feel fragile.

Where do your customers have to restart the conversation?

Caption:
More choice does not automatically create a better customer experience.

Sometimes it creates hesitation.

Iyengar and Lepper’s well-known field experiment found that a larger display attracted more attention, while the smaller selection produced more purchases. Later research showed that choice overload is not universal. It becomes more likely when the decision is difficult, the options are complex or the customer is unsure what matters.

That distinction is important.

I do not believe businesses should simply remove choices. I believe they should measure whether customers can navigate those choices confidently.

Can they understand the differences?

Does the employee ask enough questions before recommending an option?

Are comparisons clear?

Does the customer reach a decision, or leave with more uncertainty than they arrived with?

At GWR, my team observes the complete decision journey: time, questions, guidance, hesitation and outcome. That turns a vague complaint such as “the process feels complicated” into evidence that management can act on.

Choice creates value only when the customer can use it.

## Thursday, September 3 - Field File
Topic: The Choice Test
Image: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/captainredbaron/social_media/main/week-2026-09-01/thursday-field-file-choice-test.jpg

Caption:
Field File: the choice test.

When my team evaluates a journey with several options, we do not only check whether the employee described them accurately.

We observe whether the employee helped the customer decide.

Did they ask what mattered?

Did they narrow the options?

Did they explain the differences in practical terms?

Did they confirm that the recommendation matched the customer’s needs?

A complete explanation can still be a poor experience when it transfers all the decision effort back to the customer.

This is why I separate product knowledge from decision guidance. One proves that the employee knows the offer. The other proves that they can use that knowledge to help a real person move forward.

Mystery shopping makes that difference visible. We record the questions asked, the comparison provided, the recommendation made and the customer’s resulting confidence.
